In [proof theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proof_theory "Proof theory"), a branch of [mathematical logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_logic "Mathematical logic"), **proof mining** (or **proof unwinding**) is a research program that studies or analyzes formalized proofs, especially in [analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_analysis "Mathematical analysis"), to obtain explicit bounds, [ranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_logic "Linear logic") or [rates of convergence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rate_of_convergence "Rate of convergence") from proofs that, when expressed in natural language, appear to be [nonconstructive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonconstructive_proof "Nonconstructive proof").[^1] This research has led to improved results in analysis obtained from the analysis of classical proofs.
