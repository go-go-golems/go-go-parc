Superheating brake cleaner can be **seriously dangerous**, and the exact hazard depends on the formulation.

- **Chlorinated brake cleaner is the big concern.** Products containing solvents such as tetrachloroethylene/perchloroethylene can decompose on extremely hot metal, flames, or welding surfaces into **hydrogen chloride and possibly phosgene**, a highly toxic lung-damaging gas. CRC's current Brākleen SDS explicitly warns of this. citeturn453194view1turn840128view1
- **Non-chlorinated brake cleaner is often extremely flammable.** Heating it can create a readily ignitable vapor cloud, causing a flash fire or explosion. CRC, for example, classifies its non-chlorinated Brākleen as extremely flammable. citeturn579127view1
- **Heating the aerosol can itself can make it rupture.** Manufacturers warn that pressurized brake-cleaner containers may burst when heated. citeturn242661search15turn840128view0

Phosgene is especially nasty because a dangerous exposure may initially cause little irritation; severe lung injury and pulmonary edema can be **delayed for up to 48 hours**. citeturn688116view0turn688116view2

So, don't torch, weld, braze, or otherwise intensely heat a part while brake-cleaner liquid or vapor could still be present—especially with chlorinated cleaner.
