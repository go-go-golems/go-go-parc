The report is published, as a private page in the manner of your inspiration, titled "Language as the Kernel": https://claude.ai/artifact/RYA3sg8U9TeQBmA5U1cGyx

It argues that PARC, MIT and Seattle made the language the primitive, while Amoeba and Plan 9 bet on capabilities and names. JavaScript inherited the language-first bet by détournement (Scheme and Self under Java's costume), and promises and object capabilities are now carrying the unfinished distributed problem back into it.

- **The figure:** a cut-up "Naked City" map of 18 systems, from Smalltalk (1972) to ES2015 promises. A seeded walker drifts along the influences and reverses an arrow every third step. Some messages are lost in transit, a nod to Waldo's 1994 critique of treating remote calls as local. Seed, step count and rule toggles are live. It was checked in headless Chrome at desktop and phone width, with no console errors.
- **The rest:** nine theses (every third one inverted), a ledger of what each system took as its primitive, and 27 references.

Three things to know:
- **Edge types:** the map's edges are marked as documented influence, my own interpretive "rhyme" (for example Plan 9 name spaces beside SES Compartments), or critique.
- **Contested claims:** Eich's "ten days" is flagged as disputed, and E's influence on ES6 promises is attributed to Miller's own account.
- **Unchecked citations:** I added a few reference details from memory (for example the Amoeba capabilities paper's venue and the Lively book series). Check those before circulating.

Sources: Kay, Birrell & Nelson, Liskov & Shrira, the Emerald, Amoeba, Plan 9 and Self papers, Waldo et al., Miller on E, Lively Kernel papers, and Debord. The full list is in the page.
