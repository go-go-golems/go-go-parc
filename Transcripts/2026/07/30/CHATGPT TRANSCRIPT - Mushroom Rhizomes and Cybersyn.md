You’re probably combining **two 1970s ideas**:

### Project Cybersyn

The actual experiment was **Project Cybersyn**, developed in Chile under Salvador Allende from **1971 to 1973**. British cybernetician **Stafford Beer** helped design a network linking state-controlled factories by telex to computers in Santiago. Production data, shortages and other problems were meant to circulate rapidly, allowing factories to retain some autonomy while the government coordinated the wider economy. citeturn384443search0turn384443search1

It was therefore somewhat **mycelium-like**: distributed local units, continuous feedback and escalation only when a local unit could not solve a problem. But it did not use fungi, and it never controlled Chile’s entire economy. It remained a partial prototype and ended after the September 1973 military coup. citeturn976300search0turn976300news41

### Rhizomes and mycelium

A **rhizome** is technically an underground plant stem. Fungi instead form networks of filaments called **mycelium**.

The network metaphor probably comes from Gilles Deleuze and Félix Guattari’s essay **“Rhizome,” first published in 1976**. They used the rhizome as a model for decentralized, non-hierarchical organization with many possible connections and entry points. It was philosophy, however—not an implemented economic system. citeturn384443search2turn384443search15

So the name you are most likely looking for is **Project Cybersyn: Chile’s 1970s experiment in cybernetic economic coordination**. Literal computation using living fungal mycelium belongs to much later research. citeturn976300academia42
